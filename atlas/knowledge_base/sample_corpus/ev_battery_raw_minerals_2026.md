# Global EV Battery Raw Minerals & Refining Capacity Report 2026

## Executive Summary
The electric vehicle (EV) battery supply chain in 2026 is defined by severe midstream processing concentration rather than upstream mine extraction shortages. While global mining output of lithium, nickel, and cobalt has increased by 38% since 2023, chemical refining capacity into battery-grade lithium hydroxide and nickel sulfate remains heavily centralized in East Asia, representing over 72% of global conversion throughput.

## Upstream Mineral Dynamics

### Lithium (Li)
- Global lithium supply reached approximately 1.45 million metric tons of Lithium Carbonate Equivalent (LCE) in 2026.
- Australia and Chile remain the largest raw brine and spodumene ore producers, accounting for 64% of raw mineral extraction.
- However, over 70% of all spodumene concentrate must be shipped to specialized chemical refineries in China for conversion into battery-grade lithium hydroxide (LiOH) required for high-nickel NMC (811) cathodes.
- The average lead time to build and commission a greenfield chemical conversion facility outside Asia currently stands at 4.5 to 6 years due to environmental permitting and technical hydrometallurgical commissioning hurdles.

### Nickel (Ni)
- Class 1 battery-grade nickel demand accounts for 28% of total global nickel consumption in 2026.
- Indonesia has solidified its dominant position, providing over 55% of global mined nickel via High-Pressure Acid Leaching (HPAL) facilities on Sulawesi and Halmahera islands.
- Significant environmental, social, and governance (ESG) challenges persist regarding Deep Sea Tailings Placement (DSTP) and coal-powered captive energy grids, prompting US and EU automakers to demand certified low-carbon nickel.

### Cobalt (Co)
- The Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) supplies 74% of the world's raw mined cobalt.
- Automakers have accelerated the transition toward cobalt-free Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) and low-cobalt chemistries (such as NMCA and high-manganese LMFP) to reduce exposure to supply disruption and artisanal mining human rights risks.

## Midstream Cathode Active Material (CAM) Bottlenecks
- Cathode Active Material manufacturing is the single highest-value component in lithium-ion battery cells, representing 42% to 48% of total cell production cost.
- Geographic concentration in CAM manufacturing remains high: China holds 78% of global CAM output, followed by South Korea (12%) and Japan (6%).
- North American and European CAM gigafactory construction projects face high CAPEX inflationary pressures ($110M per 10 GWh vs $45M in Asia).
